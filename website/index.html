Opening it the first time: macOS will say it can't check the app for malware. That's because it isn't signed with an Apple certificate yet, not because anything is wrong with it — the source is right there to read. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway. Once only.

New York, in August: the evening fade takes 73 minutes. Midnight at the top.

Drag the slider, then change city. Same code, same thresholds — the fade gets longer as you go north because twilight does.

01

Zero blue, zero flicker

One switch. The blue channel is written as literal zeros, not merely turned down, and the backlight is pinned so nothing strobes while the dimming happens in software.

02

Follows your sky, not a timer

Type your city. It works out the sun's height through the day and warms as the light actually goes — 58 minutes at the equator, over two hours in Iceland. Fixed hours instead, if you'd rather.

03

Never touches the network

No accounts, no analytics, no update pings — not a single network call in the source, which you're welcome to go and check. Even the city list is carried inside the app, so knowing where the sun is never means telling anyone where you are.
